What is C2PA metadata?

C2PA can attach verifiable provenance information to digital content. Here is what that record can mean, and what it does not prove.

C2PA in plain English

C2PA (Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity) is a technical standard for attaching signed provenance information to digital assets. A manifest may describe an asset’s origin, edits and the tools or processes involved. Compatible apps and services can create or read these records.

Is C2PA the same as an AI label?

No. C2PA is a provenance framework, not simply an AI label. A manifest may contain information about capture, editing or creation actions. The presence or absence of a manifest alone does not establish whether an image is AI-generated or authentic.

What happens when an image is re-exported?

When an image is rendered to a fresh canvas and exported as a new file, the new file contains the rendered pixels rather than the original file container. The original embedded C2PA manifest is not carried into that browser-generated export. The original file should be retained if its provenance record is important.

What are the limitations?

Re-exporting does not remove visible labels or signals encoded into pixels. It does not forge or rewrite the original manifest; it creates a new file without the original supported metadata. Some applications or platforms may independently retain other information or analyse the image after upload.

Should you remove C2PA metadata?

That depends on your goal. If you need a smaller, clean copy for a workflow, a re-export may be useful. If provenance, creator attribution or editing history matters, keep the original and consider whether the record should remain attached when sharing.
