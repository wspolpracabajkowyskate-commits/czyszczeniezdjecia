Can a photo reveal where it was taken?

Some cameras and phones can save GPS coordinates inside a photo’s EXIF metadata. Depending on device permissions and camera settings, this may include latitude, longitude, capture time and camera details. Not every photo contains location data: GPS fields are only present when the device or app records them.

Where is photo location information stored?

Location coordinates are commonly stored in EXIF GPS fields inside the image file. They are separate from the visible pixels, so cropping or resizing the picture does not necessarily remove those fields. Messaging apps and social platforms may also process images in their own ways, so do not assume every service handles metadata identically.

What metadata cleaning cannot hide

Removing EXIF does not blur a street sign, house number, landmark, reflection or other detail visible in the image. Check the picture itself before posting. This process also does not remove invisible signals encoded into image pixels, and it cannot guarantee removal of every proprietary marker in every format.

Keep a copy of the original

Metadata can contain useful dates, camera settings, copyright and creator information. Keep your original file if you may need those details later. When image rights information matters, avoid discarding creator, credit or copyright details without considering the consequences.
